# The Stereotypical One

Ones are conscientious and ethical, with a strong sense of right and wrong. They are teachers, crusaders, and advocates for change: always striving to improve things, but afraid of making a mistake. Well-organized, orderly, and fastidious, they try to maintain high standards, but can slip into being critical and perfectionistic. They typically have problems with resentment and impatience.

> [!cite] “If you're going to do it, do it right.“

- serious and straightforward during conversation
- attuned to practicality and frugality
- intense ability to concentrate
- high internal standards

---

## Talking Style - Sermonizing

Self-Talk: _**“That’s not right.”**_

- teaching, preaching
- finding faults, correcting, reminding of obligations

---

# Where do Ones come from?

## Childhood Wounds

Ones tried hard to be good kids: often reporting that as children, they needed to “justify their existence”.

- simply being a child was somehow not acceptable,
- many developed a sense of seriousness and adult responsibility at an early age
- parents expected a lot from them, and like Threes, they often played the role of the “Family Hero”

If the protective-figure (usually the biological father) is not adequately fulfilling his role, young Ones sense a fundamental “disconnection”. They realize that their real or symbolic father does not adequately fit their temperament and needs, cauing effortless bonding to never take place.

The result is a feeling of frustration for the child and the sense that he must "father" himself. In some cases, young Ones respond to chaotic conditions around them by becoming hyperresponsible, the "voice of reason" in their families. In this way they are able to establish some sense of autonomy and boundaries - the key issues of their type.

# What makes up a One?

> [!info] Everything is connected.
The Enneagram can roughly be divided into 4 different groups, consisting of 3 subgroups with 3 types within each of them. All types are connected in some way, even if they may seem completely different at first glance.

## Intelligence Group - Body Triad

> [!tip] How Ones deal with their main emotion of **Anger** and core issue of **Wrath**.

<div>
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/triads/e189_triad.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- **Core Question:** _“Who am I, in contrast to you, and what is the space that belongs to me?”_
    - **Issue:** avoid conflict and discomfort by disengaging from personal needs and desires
- are also known as “reformer” types due to their focus on doing the right thing and perfectionistic nature.

**Ones _internalize_ Anger, in order to cope with it.**

- strive for correctness and moral purity, often directing anger inward when they or others fall short
- have a strong inner critic that pushes them to be better and uphold high standards
- seek order, fairness, and responsibility in themselves and the world around them

All Body types (Types Eight, Nine and One) depend on their instinctual intelligence to follow their "gut" and respond to threats and opportunities.

---

## Hornevian Group - Dependent Stance & Compliant Style

> [!tip] Describes how Ones get what they want and how they resolve inner conflicts.

<div>
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/triads/e126_horny.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- responsible, dedicated and want to do what's expected of them
- will do what they believe is best, even if it means sacrificing their own wants
- committed to their promises, working hard to finish what they said they would do
    - have a hard time relaxing

**These people try to obey internalized rules and principles to get what they want.**

Ones want to be good, and try to achieve this by following the rules:

1. If they act morally, correctly and responsibly, they will be worthy and avoid criticism. They may be out of touch with their own desires, so they actively look for external standards to follow.
2. If Ones make a mistake or fall short, they will work even harder to improve and correct themselves.
3. If they uphold perfection and righteousness, they can avoid blame and feel a sense of control. Ones set themselves up as superior by holding themselves (and others) to high standards.

Ones will do what they believe is right, even if it means sacrificing their comfort. Being committed to their principles, they work tirelessly to uphold order and integrity. This usually results in them having a hard time relaxing, as there is always _**something**_ to fix.

## Harmony Group - Competence

> [!tip] Describes how Ones deal with outer conflict in general.

<div>
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/triads/e135_harmony.svg" width="300" />
</div>

These types focus on logic, principles, and efficiency to solve problems. When challenges arise, the Competency types stay objective, rational, and structured, believing that problems are best addressed with clear thinking and effective solutions.

Type One will focus on rules and fairness, seeking to bring order and improvement to a situation. They strive to correct what is wrong, ensuring that standards are met and justice is upheld.

**“Following the right process will resolve the conflict.”**

While this approach brings structure and accountability, Ones may become rigid or overly critical, believing there is only one correct way forward. They can struggle with frustration when others do not meet their standards or when situations feel out of control.

Ones also have difficulties admitting to personal imperfections:

- tend to repress their anger, redirecting it into self-discipline or criticism of themselves and others
- can be so focused on doing the "right" thing that they struggle to relax or accept mistakes
- may avoid emotional vulnerability, preferring logic and duty over personal needs

## Object Relations - Idealist

> [!tip] How Ones see the world, and what they expect from relationships.

<div>
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/triads/e147_object.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- either feel inadequate or expect criticism from the start:
    - _“I must be good and correct, or I will be flawed and unworthy.”_
- they **never** want to be seen as wrong, because it might lead to failure or condemnation
    - feel the need to uphold high standards to avoid criticism and maintain worth

**Their self-value is based on mitigating imperfection.**

May have a tendency to harshly self-criticize before others can do so. Can also compensate by perfecting themselves, their work, or their environment to avoid potential flaws being exposed.

- their sense of integrity is much harder fought than what they may let others see
- their sense of self is based on countering this inadequacy by proving their righteousness

The One offers the “only” thing they can fully control: their principles. They focus on moral correctness, fairness, and improvement, believing that their worth is tied to how well they uphold what is right.

# The Paths of the One

> [!attention] Each Type in the Enneagram is connected by two lines to other types
> 
> **This can have two possible meanings:**
> 1. One line connects to the type which the person has left behind or repressed in childhood, while the second line connects to the type which the person may grow into
> 2. When we are at our best, we integrate into a “higher” type, whereas we go to a “lower” type when we’re under stress

## Path of Integration: [[Type 7 - The Adventurer]]

> [!tip] Ones become _spontaneous_ and _joyful_, like Sevens.

<div>
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/e1/e1_int.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- become more spontaneous and joyful as they integrate, embracing lightheartedness and openness
    - learn to release rigid self-control and experience life with curiosity and enthusiasm
    - develop a deeper appreciation for pleasure, play, and the present moment without guilt

- Growth does not come from simply imitating the average qualities of a Seven
    - acting impulsively or seeking constant stimulation does not resolve their core struggles
    - instead, they learn to balance discipline with flexibility, allowing themselves to make mistakes without self-condemnation
    - spontaneity, optimism, and sense of adventure of a high-functioning Seven _naturally_ unfolds

## Path of Disintegration: [[Type 4 - The Individualist]]

> [!tip] Ones become _moody_ and _irrational_, like Fours.

<div>
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/e1/e1_dis.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- Ones become more moody and irrational, taking on the emotional intensity of Fours when under stress
    - usual restraint gives way to brooding self-doubt, frustration, and emotional swings
    - may feel misunderstood, unappreciated, or resentful that their efforts go unnoticed

- Can withdraw into self-pity and become hypersensitive to criticism
    - Begin to focus on their personal suffering, seeing themselves as tragically flawed or unworthy
    - Instead of striving to fix what is wrong, they may spiral into despair, feeling like nothing will ever be good enough

- Their inner critic intensifies, making them feel both uniquely burdened and disconnected from others
    - May express emotions in erratic or dramatic ways, in contrast to their usual composed demeanor
    - Experience a deep sense of alienation, believing that no one truly understands their struggles

## Levels of Development

The Development Levels (or Health Levels) are an ever-moving system. During bad times in our lives, we will naturally drift into more unhealthy levels, eventually returning back to healthier levels once we feel better. Unlike with Integration & Disintegration, where traits are borrowed from other types, these traits originate solely from the starting type.

### Healthy Ones

> [!success] Principled reformers who embrace life's imperfections while striving for fairness and balance.
- see an abundance of situations to improve and accept the dynamic chaos of life as it is
- have a strong sense of justice and fairness, and are willing to tolerate and understand the diversity of humanity to further progress for the greater good
- achieves an ideal work-life balance and understands how to relax

### Average Ones

> [!caution] Dissatisfied with reality, they become high-minded idealists, feeling that it is up to them to improve everything.
- organize and compartmentalize all aspects of their lives, follow strict ideals and are likely passionate about various social causes
    - evident in their professional or personal pursuits and societal memberships
- often rigid workaholics, Perfectionists may suppress emotional needs in order to get things done

### Unhealthy Ones

> [!danger] Become condemnatory toward others, punitive and cruel to rid themselves of wrongdoers.
- become out of touch with reality and focus on irrelevant factors
    - self-affirming spiral of prejudice to the point of obsession and compulsion
- may discredit others’ opinions and nitpick to keep their distorted self-image in check
- little room for error in this state, and Ones can fall into explosions of rage and fury when their principles are under attack

# A Deeper Look

> [!quote] ~The Wisdom of the Enneagram
> “Simply being a child was somehow not acceptable, and many young Ones developed a sense of seriousness and adult responsibility at an early age. They understood that their parents expected a lot from them, and like Threes, they often played the role of the Family Hero. Young Ones generally take on such expectations with great earnestness.”

## Definition of Anger

Anger represents a fundamental response to perceived threats, injustice, or unmet needs. It is an intense feeling that arises when boundaries are crossed, expectations are not met, or when a person feels blocked from achieving what they desire.

The following aspects can be observed, when talking about Anger:

- **Signal of Distress**: Anger often indicates that something is wrong, whether it's an external situation or an internal conflict.
- **Desire for Change**: Anger typically arises from a desire to correct a wrong or to assert oneself in the face of opposition.

Anger typically also generates a surge of energy, which can be directed constructively or destructively. This energy can fuel determination and courage or, if uncontrolled, lead to harmful behaviors.

---

## Central Feeling of Anger & Connection to Wrath

To escape the discomfort of imperfection, Ones attempt to control themselves and their environment, striving for moral or functional excellence.

- They **internalize anger** by holding themselves to impossibly high standards, often feeling frustrated with their own perceived shortcomings.
- Over time, this unresolved anger transforms into **Wrath**-a suppressed but simmering resentment that leaks out in criticism, rigidity, or harsh self-judgment.

> [!danger] The passion of the One is Wrath: a suppressed but ever-present anger at the world's imperfections.

> _“Why can’t people just do things the right way?”_

- **Healthy Wrath** ⟶ Can be channeled into advocacy, discipline, and constructive reform.
- **Unhealthy Wrath** ⟶ Leads to bitterness, resentment, and a sense of personal failure when things are not “right.”

> [!danger] The more insecure Ones are, the more they fixate on errors-whether in themselves, others, or the world.

> _“If I don’t fix this, everything will fall apart.”_

- If no one follows their rules, they become rigid and self-righteous, believing they alone hold the moral high ground.
- If they cannot control their environment, they may retreat into inner turmoil, feeling anxious and unworthy.

Without self-compassion, Ones risk becoming trapped in cycles of perfectionism, resentment, and burnout.

---

## Balance of Perfection & Acceptance

> _“Serenity comes when we accept the world as it is - not as we wish it to be.”_

**Perfection is not the opposite of acceptance, but its obstacle. True peace is the only antidote to Wrath.**

> [!missing] **Excessive Perfectionism ⟶ Rigidity & Judgment**

Perfectionism is often a compensation for something deeper: the **underlying fear of being wrong, flawed, or unworthy.** It doesn’t always manifest as outward criticism-it can also take the form of relentless self-discipline or over-responsibility.

> [!missing] **Excessive Acceptance ⟶ Passivity & Complacency**

If Ones go too far in suppressing their drive for excellence, they may lose their sense of purpose. Accepting _everything_ without discernment can lead to apathy or inaction.

**The true balance lies in knowing when to refine and when to release.**

> [!check] Only through self-compassion and the understanding that mistakes do not define them can Ones move from resentment to serenity.

A strong indicator of this balance is their ability to **relax and trust.** When they can step away from their inner critic, they find clarity, humor, and a deeper sense of joy-realizing that life does not have to be perfect to be meaningful.

# Subtypes

## The Wings

> [!tip] Enneagram wings enrich primary type insights.
> It is believed that wings represent related personality styles which we can transition into, in order to develop new facets of ourselves. Many people identify strongly with the description of one or both of their wings, in addition to their primary type.

What mainly differentiates the wings from each other, is how they approach the common goal of the stereotypical Two: _**Connecting to and with other people.

### 1w9 - The Optimist

**Hold an “passive” approach, tend to be more introverted.**

- advocate for change and justice through teaching
- pursue what they believe is right
- make rational and objective choices
- desire love and admiration from others
- seek awareness and understanding of the world

### 1w2 - The Activist

**Hold an “active” approach, tend to be more extraverted.**

- be aware of the needs of others
- passionately follow what they believe to be right
- make principled, ethical choices
- advocate for social change and justice
- seek love, affection, and time around people

## The Instinctual Variants

> [!tip] Due to their Instinctual Variant, a type might look like another.
> It may occur that an Enneagram type appears different on the surface, because they exhibit opposing behavioural patterns than you’d might expect at first. This can be due to their Instinctual Variant - their interaction may seem like a different type, but they do not use the approach to emotions from that type.

### sp - The Detailer

> [!info] Focuses on our needs for material supplies and security, including food, warmth and family relations.

> "I'm not perfect... _yet_"

- **Countertype: turn the perfectionistic aspect upon themselves**
- believes love must be earned by doing the right thing
- feels a strong need to organize their environment, often to the point of appearing obsessive-compulsive
- high ideals, want to be decent and kind
- most self-critical of the Ones, appearing warmer and friendlier due to their repression of anger
	- sensitive to criticism, want to be safe from blame
- incredible attention to detail, may micro-manage and overengineer things

⟶ This One may resemble a Six, due to their humility, helpfulness, and worry.

### so - The Lawmaker

> [!info] Focuses on our needs for belonging and membership within the larger group and community.

> "If everyone followed the rules, the world would be a better place."

- directs their perfectionism toward others, believing they themselves are already perfect
- convinced they've "learned" the best and most correct way to live
- adopts a teacher-like, know-it-all mentality
- see themselves as "the owner of the truth", leading to rigidity and a lack of adaptability

⟶ This One may resemble a Five in their detachment or sense of authority.

### sx - The Evangelist

> [!info] Focuses on our intimate relationships & friendships, as well as the vitality of our life force.

> "You'd be a lot better, if you did what I say"

- impulse to do good but often struggles to follow through completely
- can come across as hypocritical, preaching one thing but doing another
- tends to advise others to follow their guidance, even if they don’t follow it themselves
	- may seem hypocritical, preach water but drink wine
- appears more entitled and intrusive to other people's lives

⟶ This One may resemble an Eight or a Four due to their intensity, boldness, and deep emotional investment.

> [!warning] Blind Spots  
> The instinct we neglect the most – our “blind” instinct – can create tension. The expression of this differs from where the blind spot lays.

# Growth as an One

1. **Be kinder to yourself**
	In the extreme or under stress, Type Ones can be so hard on themselves that they can make themselves miserable. Easing up on your inner negative critic is something to consider. Think about how a close friend would feel if you levelled that same critique at them.
	
2. **Recognize that not everyone is where you are**
	Ones can be great colleagues, teachers, partners and friends, but often their own high-standards can make them seem judgmental, overly rigid, and harpie-like to others! Learning to accept people as they are, and to pause (or at least censor yourself) before getting overly preachy or nitpicky is important.
	
3. **Be flexible and open to outside perspectives**
	Bringing moral clarity and strong principles to your work and life is great -- and has empowered many Type Ones who have led major social movements (like Mahatma Gandhi and Nelson Mandela). However, staying humble and grounded in living those truths (vs. just preaching them), and understanding that people may bring other views to the table, is critical to maintaining balance.
	
4. **Establish a healthy work/life balance**
	Ones tend to be driven workaholics with extraordinary focus, which can lead to much career success - as demonstrated by multiple Type Ones who have reached the heights of their own fields (from Michelle Obama, to Captain “Sully” Sullenberger, to Tina Fey). But being mindful of the need to balance all those late office nights with time for relationships, family, health, and your own overall wellness is key.
	
5. **Lighten’ up!**
	This may seem “easier said than done,” but the One's path to growth lies in learning to not take everything in life so seriously. Look for the silliness in yourself and the world around you, and take time to relax and be present.
