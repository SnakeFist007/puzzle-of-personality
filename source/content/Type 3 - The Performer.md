# The Stereotypical Three

Threes really can and do achieve great things in the world. They are the "stars" of human nature, and people often look up to them because of their graciousness and personal accomplishments. They are usually well regarded and popular among their peers, the type of person who is frequently voted “class president" or “home coming queen” because people feel they want to be associated with this kind of person who acts as a stand-in for them.

> [!cite] “Failure is not an option.“

- impressive range of accomplishments
- extremely busy and on the go
- refined taste in outer appearance
- charismatic; makes good first impressions

---

## Talking Style - Convincing

Self-Talk: _**“Watch me shine.”**_

- promoting, advertising & performing
- motivational speeches, success stories

---

# Where do Threes come from?

## Childhood Wounds

As children, Threes were not valued for themselves, but for being and doing certain things extremely well.

They learned to get validation of their worth through achievement and performance. But it never really satisfied them because it was a validation not of them but of something they had done or something they tried to become.

Threes have a very deep emotional bond with the person in the family who played the role of the nurturer (usually the mother). The child hoped this person would tell them: "You are wonderful! You are pleasing to me! You are welcomed into the world!", because they want to continue to be validated by the nurturing figure. Threes as infants learn subconsciously to adapt themselves to do and be those things that will be pleasing to that person.

Even as young adults, they may not be at all sure why they are pursuing a certain career, only that they are doing what it takes to make their family (especially their mother) proud of them. In turn they also learn to play the role of the “Family Hero”.

# What makes up a Three?

> [!info] Everything is connected.
The Enneagram can roughly be divided into 4 different groups, consisting of 3 subgroups with 3 types within each of them. All types are connected in some way, even if they may seem completely different at first glance.

## Intelligence Group - Heart Triad

> [!tip] How Threes deal with their main emotion of **Shame** and core issue of **Deceit**.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/triads/e234_triad.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- **Core Question:** _“Who do I need to become to be valued and respected?”_
    - **Issue:** not be seen, loved enough or not being cared for emotionally
- are also called “achiever” types due to their ambition, adaptability, and deep focus on success and external validation

**Threes _ignore_ Shame, in order to cope with it.**
- need to be seen as successful and accomplished to maintain their self-worth
    - will shape-shift and adapt to what is most valued in their environment to earn admiration
- seek validation through achievement and recognition

All Heart types (Types Two, Three & Four) depend on their emotional intelligence, to understand their own reactions and connect with others.

---

## Hornevian Group - Aggressive Stance & Assertive Style

> [!tip] Describes how Threes get what they want and how they resolve inner conflicts.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/horney/e378_horney.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- efficient, ambitious, and driven to succeed
- will do whatever it takes to reach their goals, even if it means suppressing their true feelings or personal needs
- highly committed to their image and achievements, working tirelessly to maintain their reputation
    - often struggle to slow down, as their sense of self-worth is tied to productivity

**These people try to achieve success and recognition to get what they want.**

Threes want admiration and status, and try to get it by excelling:

1. If they work hard, perform well, and present the right image, they will earn the admiration they crave. They may be out of touch with their authentic selves, shaping their identity around external validation.
2. If Threes do not receive enough recognition, they will push themselves even harder, optimizing their performance and refining their image.
3. If they are the best, the most successful, or the most desirable, no one will ignore or reject them. Threes set themselves up as superior by proving their competence and outshining others.

Threes will do what they believe will lead to success, even if it means sacrificing their personal wants or deeper emotions. Their commitment to achievement makes them relentless in their pursuits, often leaving little room for rest or introspection. There is always _**another goal to reach.**_

## Harmony Group - Competence

> [!tip] Describes how Threes deal with outer conflict in general.

<div>
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/triads/e135_harmony.svg" width="300" />
</div>

The task at hand is what these types focus upon. When the going gets tough, the Competency Types direct attention to efficiency, strategy, and finding the most effective solution. They believe that problems should be solved through competence, hard work, and rational decision-making.

**“Staying productive will help to resolve the conflict.”**

Type Three will focus on **performance**, ensuring that they remain successful and in control of their image, even in difficult situations. Their ability to stay composed and effective helps them maintain their status and avoid failure.

They may easily **bypass emotional struggles**, seeing vulnerability as a distraction. Their focus on productivity and results can also be a form of avoidance, so it’s important for people who react like this to sometimes slow down, acknowledge their emotions, and work through their deeper struggles with authenticity.

Threes also have difficulties admitting to personal struggles:

- Often suppress emotions in favor of maintaining a polished, capable image.
- Tend to focus on their strengths and accomplishments while **repressing feelings of failure or inadequacy**.

## Object Relations - Pragmatist

> [!tip] How Threes see the world, and what they expect from relationships.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/relation/e369_object.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- either **feel unrecognized** or expect to be **overlooked** from the start:
    - _“If I don’t prove my worth, I will be ignored.”_
- **never** want to be seen as incompetent or insignificant, because it might lead to being dismissed or losing status.
    - feel the need to **demonstrate success** in order to secure attention and validation.

**Their self-value is based on mitigating insignificance.**

May have a tendency to **overperform and overachieve** before others can ever doubt their value. Can also be compensated for by crafting an impressive image, excelling in leadership, or strategically adapting to what others admire.

- their **sense of belonging is conditional**, based on their ability to impress and succeed.
- their **sense of self is shaped by external validation**, reinforcing the belief that **who they are is what they accomplish**.

# The Paths of the Three

> [!attention] Each Type in the Enneagram is connected by two lines to other types
> 
> **This can have two possible meanings:**
> 1. One line connects to the type which the person has left behind or repressed in childhood, while the second line connects to the type which the person may grow into
> 2. When we are at our best, we integrate into a “higher” type, whereas we go to a “lower” type when we’re under stress

## Path of Integration: [[Type 6 - The Loyalist]]

> [!tip] Threes become _cooperatve_ and _committed to others_, like Sixes.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/e3/e3_int.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- become **actualized and truly authentic** by learning to value themselves beyond external success.
    - begin to recognize that **who they are is enough**, even without achievements, status, or admiration.
    - become more comfortable with **vulnerability** and allow themselves to feel and express emotions without filtering them to fit an idealized image.
- Threes will not gain much by merely imitating the average qualities of the Six.
    - becoming more anxious, doubtful, or overly reliant on external authority does little to enhance the **inner security** that Threes truly need.
    - instead, **true loyalty to themselves** develops as they learn to trust their instincts and **align their goals with their deeper values**, rather than societal expectations.
    - **courage, honesty, and community-oriented mindset** of the high-functioning Six naturally unfolds, allowing them to lead with integrity rather than image.

At their best, Threes integrate the virtues of Sixes, fostering **genuine connection, teamwork, and self-honesty**, while still maintaining their natural drive and ambition.

## Path of Disintegration: [[Type 9 - The Peacekeeper]]

> [!tip] Threes become _disengaged_ and _apathetic_, like Nines.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/e3/e3_dis.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- become increasingly **withdrawn and detached**, avoiding confrontation while privately feeling overwhelmed and insecure.
    - their usual charm and adaptability **give way to cynicism and passive-aggressiveness** as they lose confidence in their ability to maintain their image.
    - may isolate themselves, fearing exposure as a "fraud" if they can no longer uphold their polished persona.
- **self-doubt and paralysis** set in, leading to **indecisiveness and procrastination**.
    - once action-oriented, they may now hesitate, overanalyzing their choices or avoiding responsibility altogether.
    - may feel that their past successes were merely luck, leading to **imposter syndrome** and a growing fear of failure.
- usual **social fluency deteriorates**, making them appear more aloof, detached, or awkward.
    - conversations may feel more forced or stilted as they **lose confidence in their ability to "perform" well** in social situations.
    - may withdraw from relationships out of fear that others will see through them and find them lacking.

## Levels of Development

The Development Levels (or Health Levels) are an ever-moving system. During bad times in our lives, we will naturally drift into more unhealthy levels, eventually returning back to healthier levels once we feel better. Unlike with Integration & Disintegration, where traits are borrowed from other types, these traits originate solely from the starting type.

### Healthy Threes

> [!success] Charismatic achievers who inspire and drive success with adaptability, creativity, and a proactive approach.
- driven, kind, and helpful
- can push organizations to new territories with ease
- charismatic and able to recognize potential for brilliant ideas
- highly adaptable, inspiring, and creative
- has a good sense of humor
- organized, proactive, and open to feedback

### Average Threes

> [!caution] Highly concerned with their performance, constantly driving self to achieve goals as if self-worth depends on it.
- busybodies, searching for new goals to accomplish and flashy ways to flaunt their expertise
- almost always on the go with new projects to finish and people to collaborate with
- looming fear of failure propels them to keep up their momentum and continue working hard
- social media becomes a place for constant comparison and expression, which can lead to excessive self-promotional content or arrogance
- dead-set on being first place in whatever they put their minds and hearts to

### Unhealthy Threes

> [!danger] Become vindictive, attempting to ruin others’ happiness.
- jealousy and competition are common themes in their interactions
- they crave approval and reassurance from others, and without it, they tend to shut down, become lazy, unfulfilled, and may develop low self-esteem
- they may reject their sense of self, which can lead to major shifts in their career, relationship, or lifestyle, and intense mood swings
- at their worst, they ruthlessly backstab and destroy others' reputations solely for their own benefit

# A Deeper Look

> [!quote] ~The Wisdom of the Enneagram
> “As children, Threes were not valued for themselves - as very few of us were. Instead, they were valued for being and doing certain things extremely well. They learned to get validation of their worth through achievement and performance. But it never really satisfied them, because it was a validation not of them, but of something they had done.”

## Definition of Shame

Shame is an unpleasant self-conscious emotion often associated with:

- feelings of pain, exposure & distrust ⟶ **sense of powerlessness & worthlessness**
- negative self-evaluation ⟶ **motivation to quit**

It arises when we notice a substantial flaw in ourselves and we want to hide because of it. You don’t want to show yourself, or want to be seen by anyone. Examples of shame include body-image issues, heritage, being poor or having an addiction.

---

## Central Feeling of Shame & Connection to Deceit

To escape this feeling, Threes construct a **successful, competent, and admirable self-image**-attempting to become the person they believe will be most valued by others.

- Threes learn to **mask their true selves** in favor of what is most praised and rewarded in their environment.
- excel at **adapting** to different social contexts, but at the cost of losing touch with their authentic identity.

> [!danger] The passion of the Three is Deceit: not necessarily lying to others, but lying to themselves about who they truly are.

> _“I am what I achieve. If I stop achieving, I am nothing.”_

- healthy levels of Deceit allow Threes to shape-shift effectively, adapting to different challenges and excelling where needed
- unhealthy levels of Deceit lead to a **complete disconnection from their real self**, where they believe their worth is entirely dependent on external validation

> [!danger] The more insecure Threes are, the more they become performance-driven, seeking admiration rather than genuine connection.

> _“If I keep winning, you’ll have no reason to reject me.”_

- if success and recognition are lacking, Threes may **inflate** their image, exaggerate their accomplishments, or push themselves into burnout in pursuit of validation.
- if they feel unseen or undervalued, they may **resent** others for not acknowledging their efforts.
- without self-reflection, they can become overly image-conscious, believing their **persona** is who they really are.

---

## Balance of Deceit and Authenticity

> “The privilege of a lifetime is to become who you truly are.” -Carl Jung

True self-worth is not based on success, but on being **real**.

> [!missing] Excessive Deceit ⟶ Disconnection from the True Self

Threes may become so **preoccupied with their image** that they no longer know who they are beyond their achievements. This can lead to **emptiness, burnout, and a sense of never truly belonging**.

> [!missing] Excessive Authenticity ⟶ Fear of Losing Status

As Threes begin to let go of their masks, they may **fear losing their edge**-worried that if they stop performing, they will no longer be valued. However, **genuine self-worth does not depend on external validation**.

> [!check] Only by embracing their real feelings, strengths, and limitations-without needing to impress-can Threes experience true fulfillment.

# Subtypes

## The Wings

> [!tip] Enneagram wings enrich primary type insights.
> It is believed that wings represent related personality styles which we can transition into, in order to develop new facets of ourselves. Many people identify strongly with the description of one or both of their wings, in addition to their primary type.

What mainly differentiates the wings from each other, is how they approach the common goal of the stereotypical Three:

### 3w2 - The Enchanter

**Hold an “active” approach, tend to be more extraverted.**

- Be encouraging and vivacious
- Enjoy setting ambitious goals
- Seek success and accomplishment
- Focus on their social appearance
- Fear being rejected or unworthy of love

### 3w4 - The Expert

**Hold an “passive” approach, tend to be more introverted.**

- Be more restrained and controlled than other three types
- Have a basic fear of being unworthy
- Desire success and affirmation
- Think and process internally
- Focus on their professional development

---

## The Instinctual Variants

> [!tip] Due to their Instinctual Variant, a type might look like another.
> It may occur that an Enneagram type appears different on the surface, because they exhibit opposing behavioural patterns than you’d might expect at first. This can be due to their Instinctual Variant - their interaction may seem like a different type, but they do not use the approach to emotions from that type.

### sp - The Worker

> [!info] Focuses on our needs for material supplies and security, including food, warmth and family relations.

> "I need to be solid."

- **Countertype:**
- quality oriented: test their own work & relationships to see if they are worthy
- want to set examples & standards, be a good role-model / the ideal way to be
- strives for efficiency and are detail-oriented
- works towards security and stability
- workaholic: may become anxious when not working and may have difficulty maintaining intimate relationships
    - sacrifice relationships and health for job security and money

⟶ This Three may resemble a One or a Six due to their modesty, duty-focus, and anxiety around being “enough”.

### so - The Politician

> [!info] Focuses on our needs for belonging and membership within the larger group and community.

> "Success is everything."

- need recognition and reassurance that they are making progress
- strongly identify with their social roles and want the right credentials
- become more driven in their social ambitions as anxiety increases, with a desire for fame
- may become desperate for attention and can become dishonest in their pursuit of recognition

⟶ This is the “classic” Three!

### sx - The Charismatic

> [!info] Focuses on our intimate relationships & friendships, as well as the vitality of our life force.

> "See how special I am..."

- have a strong desire to be desired, wanting to be valued and wanted by others
- develop an appealing image, also help others maximize their attractiveness
- desire to impress by drawing attention to their own exceptional qualities
- can be seductive but often fear they won't be able to live up to the image they are projecting
- possess a strong desire for intimacy, but fear deep emotional connection

⟶ This Three may resemble a Two or a Seven, due to their emotional hunger and seductive expressiveness.

### Blind Spot

> [!warning] Blind Spots  
> The instinct we neglect the most – our “blind” instinct – can create tension. The expression of this differs from where the blind spot lays.

# Growth as a Three

1. **Slow down!**
	It can be easy for a Three to get so busy that they can miss what is right in front of them or take the present moment for granted. Take time to relax and appreciate the moment every once in a while. (Yes, that includes turning off your smartphone!)
	
2. **Practice active listening to connect more deeply with other people**
	Threes can be so focused on how they are being perceived by other people, that they miss opportunities to connect with others more deeply. By developing active listening habits, Threes can form deeper bonds with others.
	
3. **Embrace vulnerability**
	Vulnerability can be scary for a Three, because it means allowing other people to see who you truly are, not just the side of you that you want them to see. However, Threes grow when they learn how to embrace vulnerability and act in ways that are more authentic to themselves.
	
4. **Examine your thoughts and feelings**
	Threes can get so caught up in their image and achievements that they ignore their own emotions. Take time to ask yourself how you really feel about something. Do your words and actions match up with how you feel?
	
5. **Practice mindfulness to be more present**
	Threes are always on-the-go. Mindfulness practices like yoga and meditation can help you slow down and focus on the present. Non-competitive, single-player games can also help Threes focus on the moment without feeling the need to “win.”
