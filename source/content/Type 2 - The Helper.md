# The Stereotypical Two

Being generous and going out of their way for others makes Twos feel that theirs is the richest, most meaningful way to live. The love and concern they feel - and the genuine good they do - warms their hearts and makes them feel worthwhile. Twos are most interested in what they feel to be the “really, really good” things in life - love, closeness, sharing, family, and friendship.

> [!cite] “What do you need help with?”

- strangers are naturally drawn to them for directions and advice
- strong aura of approachability
- highly attuned to the needs of others
- continually make efforts to keep in touch with loved ones

---

## Talking Style - Warmth

Self-Talk: _**"You need me.”**_

- befriending, supporting & comforting
- offering friendly advice, pitching in
- meeting needs, getting personal

---

# Where do Twos come from?

## Childhood Wounds

Twos come to believe three things during childhood:

1. they must _put other people’s needs ahead_ of their own
2. they must _give_ love, in order to _receive_ it
3. they must _earn_ a place in the affections of others, because love will not simply be given to them

Twos are trying to fix in other the hurts that they are unable to fully acknowledge in themselves. They learn to deal with their negative feelings by focusing on others, trying hard to please and help them. Ultimately, they will do almost anything to get some sign, some token, that they are loved. To acknowledge their own needs is a form of selfishness and forbidden by their superego.

- deeply conditions them to believe, that by sacrificing themselves they will be rewarded with whatever is called _love_ in their family system
- increasing difficulty acknowledging their own needs and pain ⟶ are drawn automatically to the needs and pain they see in others

# What makes up a Two?

> [!info] Everything is connected.
> The Enneagram is a multifaceted system with several different frameworks or “schools of thought” that describe personality types from unique angles. While these models sometimes emphasize different aspects and may seem to contradict each other, they all provide valuable insights to help identify and understand your core type.

## Intelligence Group - Heart Triad

> [!tip] How Twos deal with their main emotion of **Shame** and core issue of **Pride**.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/triads/e234_triad.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- **Core Question:** _“With who am I in a relationship of some sorts, and what quality does this relationship have?”_
    - **Issue:** not be seen, loved enough or not being cared for emotionally
- are also called “helper” types due to their nurturing nature and deep focus on forming supportive relationships

**Twos _externalize_ Shame, in order to cope with it.**

- need enough feedback that they’re of value to someone, to soothe their soul
    - will do whatever they can to get that feedback and are often totally focused on getting others needs met while neglecting their own
- seek connection with others

All Heart types (Types Two, Three & Four) depend on their emotional intelligence, to understand their own reactions and connect with others.

---

## Hornevian Group - Dependent Stance & Compliant Style

> [!tip] Developed by Karen Horney, it describes how Twos get what they want and how they resolve inner conflicts.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/horney/e126_horney.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- responsible, dedicated and want to do what's expected of them
- will do what they believe is best, even if it means sacrificing their own wants
- committed to their promises, working hard to finish what they said they would do
    - have a hard time relaxing

**These people try to obey internalized rules and principles to get what they want.**

Twos want attention, and try to get it by following the rules:

1. If they love, help, and care enough for others, they will eventually earn attention from them. They may be out of touch with their inner needs, so they actively look for value systems to guide and help them.
2. If Twos do not receive enough attention, they will increase the amount of help they give.
3. If they are all-loving saints, no one will reject them, and they will be loved in return. Twos set themselves up as superior by making others dependent on them.

Twos will do what they believe is best, even if it means sacrificing their own wants. Being committed to their promises, they work hard to finish what they said they would do. This usually results in them having a hard time relaxing, as there is always _**something**_ to do.

## Harmony Group - Positive Outlook

> [!tip] Describes the Twos general approach to conflict and outer challenges.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/harmony/e279_harmony.svg" width="300" />
</div>

The silver lining is what these types focus upon. When the going gets tough, the Positive Outlook types bring our attention to what is still going right and how the problem may not be that bad.
Type Two will focus on people, calming and reassuring them in trouble, which also helps them to keep their own inner peace.

**“Staying positive will help to resolve the conflict.”**

Because this is used as a defense mechanism, they may easily overlook a problem, or even denying its existence. The focus on happiness and positivity can also be used as avoidance, so it’s important for people who react like this to sometimes face an obstacle as it is, working through the pain and difficulty with resilience.

Twos also have difficulties admitting to problems:

- in turn also have issues balancing their needs with the needs of others
- tend to focus on their own positive qualities, while repressing their negative thoughts

## Object Relations - Relationalist

> [!tip] How Twos form their sense of self through relationships.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/relation/e258_object.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- either feel rejected or expect rejection from the get-go:
    - _“others don't care about my needs”_

- they **never** want to be seen as a burden, because it might lead to rejection
    - feel the need to offer _something_, in order to avoid future rejection

**Their self-value is based on mitigating rejection.**

May have a tendency to pre-reject themselves before others can do so in social situations. Can also be compensated for by making themselves indispensable by giving, leading or adding knowledge to the environment.

- their sense of belonging is much harder fought, than what they may have others believe
- their sense of self is based on countering this rejection by offering their talent

The Two offers the “only” gift it has left: their heart. They focus on the needs of others and help, care for, and nurture them.

# The Paths of the Two

> [!attention] Each Type in the Enneagram is connected by two lines to other types
> 
> **This can have two possible meanings:**
> 1. One line connects to the type which the person has left behind or repressed in childhood, while the second line connects to the type which the person may grow into
> 2. When we are at our best, we integrate into a “higher” type, whereas we go to a “lower” type when we’re under stress

## Path of Integration: [[Type 4 - The Individualist]]

> [!tip] Prideful Twos become _self-nurturing_, like Fours.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/e2/e2_int.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- become actualized and remain healthy by learning to recognize and accept all of their feelings without censoring
    - become acquainted and comfortable with the entire panoply of feeling states available to them - including their secret needs and their darkest hatreds

- will not gain much by attempting to imitate the average qualities of the Four
    - being more emotionally volatile and self-absorbed does little to enhance the real self-knowledge that Twos need
    - superego is broken down, restrictions against "selfishness" are resolved and they learn to genuinely nurture themselves
    - the self-knowledge, self-revelation, and creativity of the high-functioning Four _naturally_ unfolds

## Path of Disintegration: [[Type 8 - The Challenger]]

> [!tip] Desperate Twos become _aggressive_ and _dominating_, like Eights.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/e2/e2_dis.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- become more blunt and forceful, which reveals that they are remarkably tough underneath it all
    - “Beneath the velvet glove is an iron fist.”
- can be surprisingly aggressive and argumentative, insisting quite strongly that they have been wronged in some way
- usual indirectness shifts into a more frontal approach, in which average Twos confront people directly about their lack of response
    - complaining about not being given an expected term of endearment or sufficient appreciation
    - do not want their efforts to go unrecognized, put people on notice as to who is running things
- make threats and undermine the confidence of the people who need them

## Path of Health Levels

The Development Levels (also known as Health Levels) are an ever-moving system.

During bad times in our lives, we will naturally drift into more unhealthy levels, eventually returning back to healthier levels once we feel better. Unlike with Integration & Disintegration, where traits are borrowed from other types, these traits originate solely from the starting type.

### Healthy Twos

> [!success] Selfless caregivers who are fulfilled by freely giving unconditional love.
- comfortable with sharing their own needs to others and secure a healthy give-and-take balance
- recognize their own self-worth and can gently guide others towards theirs as well
- acknowledge when others require distance and develop secure attachment styles
- able to have genuine, heart-to-heart connections with others

### Average Twos

> [!caution] Constantly seek ways to feel important by attending to others’ needs.
- feel fulfilled when others remind them how grateful they are to have them in their lives
- work hard to maintain the image of the constantly accessible friend
- use flattery or compliments in order to gain acceptance and appreciation
- hypersensitive to approval and appraisal of others, especially those they truly care about

### Unhealthy Twos

> [!danger] Pit of self-despair and criticism - constantly looking for others’ faults.
- try to gain control over relationships, may become overly clingy or overbearing
- have an excuse for their every action, play the victim card in order to gain sympathy and reassurance
- may mindlessly blame other people for their suffering and misery
- if received with criticism, wallow in despair and develop various forms of physical illness

# A Deeper Look

> [!quote] The Wisdom of the Enneagram
> “When our authentic, essential qualities are not mirrored in early childhood, we come to the conclusion that something is wrong with us. The resulting feeling is shame. The loving self-image of Twos covers deep feelings of shame, grief, and hostility. As long as these feelings are unprocessed, Twos cannot express all of their feelings.”

## Definition of Shame

Shame is an unpleasant self-conscious emotion often associated with:

- feelings of pain, exposure & distrust ⟶ **sense of powerlessness & worthlessness**
- negative self-evaluation ⟶ **motivation to quit**

It arises when we notice a substantial flaw in ourselves and we want to hide because of it. You don’t want to show yourself, or want to be seen by anyone. Examples of shame include body-image issues, heritage, being poor or having an addiction.

---

## Central Feeling of Shame & Connection to Pride

To escape this feeling, Twos attempt to feel valuable by means of their self-image - trying to be caring and of service to others.
- Twos deny their own needs while attempting to “help” others

Pride both **prevents** them from experiencing love and nurturance from others, and **diverts** them from ever healing the wounds disguised by their (seemingly) selfless actions.

> [!danger] The passion of the two is Pride: an expression of the unwillingness to acknowledge their own hurt.

> _“I don't need anything, I'm fine. I'm here to take care of you!”_

- healthy levels of Pride can show your true strengths ⟶ “intellectual honesty about what strengths you possess”
- unhealthy levels of Pride can have an excessive or pretentious show of pride in their own achievements

> [!danger] The more insecure Twos are, the more they tend to flatter others with the hope that they will be thanked and flattered themselves.

> *“If it weren't for me, where would you be?”*

- if no one gives them this praise, they start to manipulate people, by finding out others’ needs and desires, and creating dependencies
    - if that also fails, they make others feel unworthy of their love, affection and attention

- missing self-criticism also leads to exaggeration of their accomplishments
    - sense of greatness & superiority, which shows itself in dramatic displays of their own emotions or the rush for activities, where the Two is the center of attention

---

## Balance of Pride and Humility

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/e2/balance.gif" width="300" />
</div>

> “Pride is not the opposite of shame, but its source. True humility is the only antidote to shame.” ~Iroh

Recognition that their abilities are gifts & respect for others that are _as_ or even _more_ gifted than they are.

> [!missing] Excessive Pride ⟶ Arrogance

Pride is a compensation for something else: the underlying fear of worthlessness and being alone. It doesn't necessarily come in the form of “proud thoughts” or arrogant expression, it can also come in the form of false humility.

> [!missing] Excessive Humility ⟶ Sense of Worthlessness

Their sense of humor is an indication of how good this balance is. The ability to laugh at oneself requires them to recognize the limitations of their abilities and have the self-confidence to accept them.

> [!check] Only true humility and the knowledge that we are loved, without needing to give, will dissolve pride.

# Variants

## The Wings

> [!tip] Wings are personality styles directly adjacent to your core type.
> They offer nuance and flexibility in how a type expresses itself – often felt as secondary influences you lean on or grow into.

What mainly differentiates the wings from each other, is how they approach the common goal of the stereotypical Two: _**Connecting to and with other people.**_

### 2w1 - The Servant

**Hold an “passive” approach, tend to be more introverted.**

- constantly uphold an aura of approachability and friendliness
- critical and harsh with themselves
- morality with empathy: “doing the right thing for the right people”

### 2w3 - The Host

**Hold an “active” approach, tend to be more extraverted.**

- bend themselves to achieve the image expected from others
- enjoy making others feel better, but also command more attention for themselves
- may be focused on image & personal gain

---

## The Instinctual Variants

> [!tip] Due to their Instinctual Variant, a type might look like another.
> It may occur that an Enneagram type appears different on the surface, because they exhibit opposing behavioural patterns than you’d might expect at first. This can be due to their Instinctual Variant - their interaction may seem like a different type, but they do not use the approach to emotions from that type.

 At their core, all variants still share the essential drive of Type 2: to feel loved and needed.

### sp - The Anchor

> [!info] Focused on physical needs, safety, and self-maintenance - food, shelter, health, personal boundaries.

> "Look at what **I** have done for **you**"

- Tends to focus love through **acts of service** or **providing material support**.
- Their helpfulness may have an underlying tone of **“I take care of you, so you take care of me.”**
- Can seem more **introverted** or **reserved** than expected - may not like being in charge, but values **dependability and loyalty**.
- Struggles with taking up space or asking for help openly - indirect in their needs.
- Might distract themselves with practicalities when emotional issues feel overwhelming.

⟶ This Two may resemble a Six or a One in their groundedness or anxiety about being needed.

### so - The Networker

> [!info] Focused on group dynamics, belonging, social recognition, and influence.

> "Everybody's friend"

- Expresses love by **connecting, organizing, and improving social systems** - being the center of the network.    
- Wants to be seen as capable, informed, and **someone others admire and rely on**.
- Motivated to lead and influence, but may frame it as being helpful to the group.
- Has an internal fear of being left out or not being **seen as important** by others.
- The “giver” role is used to build loyalty and reputation.

⟶ This Two might appear similar to a Three or an Eight in their drive and social charisma.

### sx - The Intensifier

> [!info] Focused on intensity, connection, one-on-one bonds, and passionate engagement with life.

> "**Your** best friend"

- Expresses love by forming deep, **exclusive emotional bonds**.
- Wants to be **irreplaceable** - someone’s closest person, their emotional anchor.
- Often seductive, charming, expressive, and generous with affection.
- Has an intuitive sense of what others desire - and how to make them feel special.
- Their attention is **highly focused** on key people and emotionally charged interactions.  

⟶ This Two may resemble a Four or a Seven in their intensity, expressiveness, and idealism in relationships.

### Blind Spot

> [!warning] Blind Spots  
> The instinct we neglect the most – our “blind” instinct – can create tension. The expression of this differs from where the blind spot lays.

# Growth as a Two

1. **Practice mindfulness for Personal Growth**
	When Twos learn how to step outside of themselves and see their personality more objectively, they can observe patterns in their thoughts, feelings, and behaviors. This way, they can better understand where they need to set boundaries and practice self-care.
	
2. **Take note of your feelings about things**
	Growth for a Type Two lies in acknowledging and accepting their own emotions, instead of denying them and prioritizing others all the time. Activities like journaling can help Twos get more in touch with their own emotions.
	
3. **Learn how to set healthy boundaries**
	They are known for giving more energy to others than they may actually have to give. This leaves them feeling depleted and detached from their own identity. Twos flourish when they learn how to set healthy boundaries focused on their needs.
	
4. **Practice self-love**
	They often feel trapped in their need to please other people. If approval is not easy to gain, the Twos may feel unworthy because they are not appreciated. It’s essential for Twos to learn to love who **they** truly are.
	
5. **Share the authentic self with other people**
	They tend to alter their personalities to please the people around them. Make a conscious effort to be transparent and honest about how you think and feel. You will learn that people truly value the authentic side of you!
