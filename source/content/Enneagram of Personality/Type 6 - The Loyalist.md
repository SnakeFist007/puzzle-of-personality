# The Stereotypical Six

Sixes are known for their loyalty to friends, beliefs, and even ideas. They will stick with relationships and beliefs even when others have given up. They will fight for their beliefs and defend their community or family more than they will defend themselves.

> [!cite] “Can we be friends?“

- reliable, responsible & trustworthy
- able to foresee problems and foster cooperation
- but can also become defensive, evasive & anxious
- typically have problems with self-doubt and suspicion
    - at their best, however, they are internally stable and self-reliant, courageously championing themselves and others

---

## Talking Style - Apologetic

Self-Talk: _**“But what if…”**_

- questions, warnings & second-guessing
- troubleshooting worst-case scenarios, defending & testing

---

# Where do Sixes come from?

## Childhood Wounds

**Separation Phase**

At a certain point in their development toddlers begin, despite their tremendous dependency, to move away from their mothers, to assert their independence and autonomy

A key-figure for this courage of seperation is the presence of the father-figure.

- provides discipline, structure and authority
- if present in a strong and consistent way, he provides guidance and support for the child

For most this process goes less than perfectly though, resulting in insecurities as adults. Sixes however, are particularly fixated on it.

Sixes long for approval and closeness, but feel the need to defend against it at the same time. They want to be supported, but not overwhelmed.

Many Sixes end up in an uneasy compromise:

- offer outward obedience yet retain a feeling of independence through inward rebellion and cynicism
- may also act in small or large acts of passive-aggression

# What makes up a Six?

> [!info] Everything is connected.
The Enneagram can roughly be divided into 4 different groups, consisting of 3 subgroups with 3 types within each of them. All types are connected in some way, even if they may seem completely different at first glance.

## Intelligence Group - Head Triad

> [!tip] How Sixes deal with their main emotion of **Fear** and core issue of **Doubt**.

- **Core Question:** _“When will I know that I have enough security?”_
    - **Issue:** fear being abandoned and left without support
- are also called “loyalty types”, reflecting their commitment to security, support, and community

**Sixes _ignore_ Fear, in order to cope with it.**

- push fears away, in order to not be overwhelmed by them

All Head types (Types Five, Six and Seven) depend on their intellectual intelligence to make sense of things and navigate the world around them.

## Hornevian Group - Dependent Stance & Compliant Style

> [!tip] Describes how Sixes get what they want and how they resolve inner conflicts.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/2025-04-08-Type_126_Horney-1.svg" width="300" />
</div>

**These people try to obey internalized rules and principles to get what they want.**

Sixes want security, and try to get it by following the rules. If Sixes are sufficiently dedicated and committed to an authority figure, they will earn security from it. If they do not receive enough security, they will either strengthen their commitment to their current authority figure, or will rebel against it and seek it from another source.

Their commitment to their cause gives them confidence and makes them look down upon those with different beliefs.

## Harmony Group - Reactivity

> [!tip] Describes how Sixes deal with outer conflict in general.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/2025-04-08-Type_468_Harmony-1.svg" width="300" />
</div>

Sixes can get into conflict by being too pessimistic, suspicious, and doubtful. They tend to be very anxious people, always on the watch for dangers and threats. When conflicts or threats occur, their anxiety comes out, often as long rants, hysterical overreactions, and feelings of inferiority. Sixes want to trust others, but remain wary of problems.

**They need feedback to see where they stand with others.**

Sometimes they let their guard down (when within the confines of the authority / belief). Other times, they keep their guard up to ensure that others do not take advantage of them.

# The Paths of the Six

> [!attention] Each Type in the Enneagram is connected by two lines to other types
> 
> **This can have two possible meanings:**
> 1. One line connects to the type which the person has left behind or repressed in childhood, while the second line connects to the type which the person may grow into
> 2. When we are at our best, we integrate into a “higher” type, whereas we go to a “lower” type when we’re under stress

## Path of Integration: [[Type 9 - The Peacekeeper]]

> [!tip] Sixes become _relaxed_ and _optimistic_, like Nines.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/2025-04-08-Type_6_Integration-1.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- to find stability, focus on physical presence to get grounded in the present moment
- being active doesn't equate to being aware of the body's sensations. Pay attention to sensory impressions to counterbalance constant thinking and provide an alternative identity
- this helps to develop trust in life and the ground beneath us. It also fills us with genuine courage to face challenges and threats with inner balance

## Path of Disintegration: [[Type 3 - The Performer]]

> [!tip] Sixes become _competitive_ and _arrogant_, like Threes.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/2025-04-08-Type_6_Disintegration-1.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- may also become boastful and self-promoting
- dismissing others, and hyping their own superiority in a desperate attempt to defend against their low self-esteem and feelings of inferiority
- dishonesty about their background or education, exploitation of selfor others, and a relentless desire to triumph over rival groups or ideologies can enter the picture

## Levels of Development

The Development Levels (or Health Levels) are an ever-moving system. During bad times in our lives, we will naturally drift into more unhealthy levels, eventually returning back to healthier levels once we feel better. Unlike with Integration & Disintegration, where traits are borrowed from other types, these traits originate solely from the starting type.

### Healthy Sixes

> [!success] Reliable team players who handle stress well and embrace independence.
- caring team players who positively influence colleagues and friends
- hardworking employees who take pride in their work and strive to improve their skills
- skilled at handling stress and diffusing tension
- develop secure attachment styles and trust others easily
- learn to embrace their independence and express themselves freely

### Average Sixes

> [!caution] Resist having more demands made on them, react against others passive-aggressively.
- mentally acute yet highly skeptical, and seek security and group approval for their actions
- become aware of their shortcomings and their self-esteem may fluctuate from time to time
- may mentally prepare themselves with the worse case scenario before carrying out a task
- have a tendency to over-analyze messages or information as it comes in, which further fuels their anxiety - and leads to mixed signals
    - confuses others, so the whole thing winds up being a vicious cycle of worry

### Unhealthy Sixes

> [!danger] Feeling persecuted, that others are “out to get them,” they lash-out and act irrationally, bringing about what they fear.
- become extremely paranoid and suspicious of everything and everyone in their path
- prone to developing anxiety due to their hypervigilance to any impending (real or imagined) sources of harm
- can develop overwhelming and debilitating anxiety from the illusion of constant danger
- when exasperated and fed up, Sixes will project their insecurities onto others and claim they’ve done something, when it was all brewed up and imagined from the beginning

# A Deeper Look

> [!quote] ~The Wisdom of the Enneagram
> “Sixes learn to cope with fear either by reacting with it or against it. Some Sixes express themselves more aggressively, while others are more visibly timid.“

## Definition of Fear

Fear is a fundamental emotional response that arises from a deep sense of insecurity about one's ability to cope with the world. It's not just the typical fear of specific things or situations, but a more pervasive and often subconscious feeling that:

- influences a person's worldview & behavior
- influences interactions with others

---

## Central Feeling of Fear & Connection to Doubt

Sixes often view the world as a potentially threatening place. This perception fuels their underlying fear, leading them to constantly question and doubt their environment and the intentions of others. They seek security and certainty to counterbalance this fear. Doubt serves as a defense mechanism for Sixes. By constantly questioning and seeking assurance, they attempt to protect themselves from perceived threats. This skepticism, however, can sometimes become paralyzing, preventing them from taking action or fully trusting others.

1. **Fear-Driven Behaviors**: Their fear often leads to behaviors aimed at mitigating risks, such as seeking constant validation, preparing for worst-case scenarios, and being overly cautious. These behaviors are rooted in the doubt that they can handle situations without extensive preparation or that others can be relied upon.
2. **Doubt in Relationships**: In relationships, Type 6s may struggle with trust due to their fear and doubt. They might constantly seek reassurance from their partners or friends, doubting their loyalty or intentions. This can create tension and prevent the formation of deep, trusting connections.

---

## Coping with Fear and Doubt

Growth involves recognizing and addressing their fears and doubts. This might include practices like mindfulness to stay present and reduce anxiety, building trust in relationships, and challenging their skepticism by taking calculated risks. By confronting their fear and learning to manage their doubt, Type 6s can find a more balanced and secure way to engage with the world.

# Subtypes

## The Wings

> [!tip] Enneagram wings enrich primary type insights.
> It is believed that wings represent related personality styles which we can transition into, in order to develop new facets of ourselves. Many people identify strongly with the description of one or both of their wings, in addition to their primary type.

What mainly differentiates the wings from each other, is how they approach the common goal of the stereotypical Six: **Safety and stability, as well as assurance and predictability**

### 6w5 - The Guardian

- are hard-working and intellectual
- fear losing their stability
- project their feelings onto others
- think logically and analytically

### 6w7 - The Confidant

- love being around people
- are afraid of losing their security
- make jokes to deflect and project feelings
- value reliability and trust in relationships

## The Instinctual Variants

> [!tip] Due to their Instinctual Variant, a type might look like another.
> It may occur that an Enneagram type appears different on the surface, because they exhibit opposing behavioural patterns than you’d might expect at first. This can be due to their Instinctual Variant - their interaction may seem like a different type, but they do not use the approach to emotions from that type.

### sp - The Guardian

> [!info] Focuses on our needs for material supplies and security, including food, warmth and family relations.

<mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6; color: #ffffff;">May seem like e2 or e7</mark>

> "I need to have the right alliances."

- lead with warmth and kindness, seek connections and alliances
- easy-going, active people
- loyal followers, but suspicious on the inside
    - scan their environment for inconsistencies, may test people
- indecisive at times, never feel like they have enough proof for something
- will check-in with people regularly, if they really like them
- may be hard for them to apologise
- have a hard time solving problems on their own, even though they're very capable of that
    - need outside support to get to a conclusion, to really feel sure
- can bring doubt into situations, where they wasn't any in the first place

### so - The Loyalist

> [!info] Focuses on our needs for belonging and membership within the larger group and community.

<mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6; color: #ffffff;">May seem like </mark>

> "I need to follow the rules."

- may struggle with personal success and development
- cope their anxiety through rules (religion, constitutions for example)
    - fear losing support from the group or authority due to their indecisiveness
- follow the rules to get to safety
    - fears doing the wrong thing, in the eyes of their authoritative figure
    - get their identity and duty from the autority figure
- want the world to be black & white, so they see dangers ahead of time
- provoke critical thinking through doubts

### sx - The Rebel

> [!info] Focuses on our intimate relationships & friendships, as well as the vitality of our life force.

<mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6; color: #ffffff;">May seem like e8 or e4</mark>

> "I’m going to be strong, so they won’t attack me."

- **Countertype:**
- may develop physical strength, power and/or attractiveness to feel safe
    - may look courageous and not afraid at all
    - wants to be strong, instill fear into others (through doubt / anxiety)
- mask insecurities through open assertion, defiance of authority or flirtation and seduction
- more openly defiant of authority when anxious
    - most doubtful of others and themselves
    - may assert themselves against their own supporters or third parties instead of the true source of their anxieties
- believe anybody can be a threat, have a hard time trusting and believing in others

# Growth as a Six

1. **Own your power**
	Sixes tend to view life as a series of events that happens to them, rather than a journey where they steer the ship. When Sixes learn to acknowledge and own the power they yield, they can be braver and bolder in how they show up to the world.

2. **Be aware of how you create self-fulfilling prophecies**
	A self-fulfilling prophecy happens when you unconsciously make something happen by over-fixating on it. Sixes do this when they allow their fear to lead to extreme paranoia. For example, if a Six over-fixates on the fear that their partner is less interested in them, they may introduce added tension to the relationship that wouldn’t be there otherwise.

3. **Work on building trust in your relationships**
	Understand that not everyone has ulterior motives or is out to harm you. This kind of scepticism can cause problems in otherwise healthy relationships. When Sixes learn to have more faith in the people in their lives, they can form stronger and more meaningful connections.

4. **Develop healthy habits to release anxiety**
	Sixes spend a lot of time in their heads, so it’s essential to practice habits that allow your energy to focus on your body. While exercise is important for everyone, it’s especially helpful in getting a Six to focus more on the present and release stress.

5. **Embrace Grounding and Trust**
	Practice grounding techniques, confront recurring fears, and question their validity. Shift focus from negativity to positivity, and embrace fearful situations to build courage and foster openheartedness in life and relationships.
