# The Stereotypical Eight

Eights enjoy taking on challenges themselves as well as giving others opportunities that challenge them to exceed themselves in some way. Eights are charismatic and have the physical and psychological capacities to persuade others to follow them into all kinds of endeavors - from starting a company, to rebuilding a city, to running a household, to waging war, to making peace.

> [!cite] “I will make this happen, no matter what.“

- independent and self-sufficient
- very energetic and busy
- fierce and confident look, determined & passionate
- serious about control over environment, stubborn

---

## Talking Style - Commanding

Self-Talk: _**“Do this my way.”**_

- arguing, debating & opposing
- taking aim & parting shots
- military metaphors

# Where do Eights come from?

## Childhood Wounds

Most Eights have told us that they felt that they had to become "adults" at an early age, perhaps to help bring in money to raise the other children in the family because of an absent father or some other calamity.

They may have had to deal with a dangerous environment (such as drug dealers, or street gangs, or some kind of war zone), or with an erratic or violent adult in their home. Other Eights grow up in fairly normal families but may have felt the need to protect their feelings for other reasons.

Eights tend to grow up quickly, and survival issues are foremost to them, as if they were asking: "How can I - and the few people I care about - survive in a cruel, uncaring world?”

---

# What makes up a Eight?

> [!info] Everything is connected.
The Enneagram can roughly be divided into 4 different groups, consisting of 3 subgroups with 3 types within each of them. All types are connected in some way, even if they may seem completely different at first glance.

## Intelligence Group - Body Triad

> [!tip] How Eights deal with their main emotion of **Anger** and core issue of **Lust**.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/2025-04-08-Type_189_Triad-1.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- **Core Question:** _“Who am I, in contrast to you, and what is the space that belongs to me?”_
    - **Issue:** avoid conflict and discomfort by disengaging from personal needs and desires
- are also known as “challengers”, due to their assertiveness, intensity, and drive to protect themselves and others

**Eights _externalize_ Anger, in order to cope with it.**

- tend to react quickly and forcefully to perceived threats or injustices
    - anger is used as a shield - a way to stay in control, assert boundaries, and avoid vulnerability
- operate from a place of high energy, intensity, and gut instinct - pushing into life headfirst

All Body types (Types Eight, Nine and One) depend on their instinctual intelligence to follow their "gut" and respond to threats and opportunities.

For Eights, anger is not hidden or denied - it’s a vital signal that something is wrong, and it propels them to act. They channel this energy into protecting themselves and the people or causes they care about, often stepping into leadership or advocacy roles. Beneath the surface, however, lies a core sensitivity to being betrayed or controlled - which can fuel their drive to stay strong and never show weakness.

## Hornevian Group - Aggressive Stance & Aggressive Style

> [!tip] Describes how Eights get what they want and how they resolve inner conflicts.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/2025-04-08-Type_378_Horney-1.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- direct, self-confident, and action-oriented
- move _against_ obstacles and take charge of situations
- dislike feeling controlled or dependent on others
    - will often test boundaries - theirs and yours - to see who’s in charge

**These people assert their will to shape the world and protect their autonomy.**

Eights want control and impact, and try to get it by leading from the front:

1. If they assert themselves boldly and decisively, they believe they can shape outcomes and avoid being hurt. They often trust action more than vulnerability.
2. If they sense weakness, manipulation, or threat in others, they may become confrontational or challenging - testing others to gauge trust and strength.
3. If they maintain a tough exterior, they believe they can survive and protect those they care about. But this can isolate them emotionally, cutting them off from their own softer side.

Eights meet life head-on. They resolve conflict by confronting it directly, often with force or intensity. While this gives them a powerful presence, it can also intimidate others or mask their underlying sensitivity - especially their fear of betrayal or loss of control.

---

## Harmony Group - Reactivity

> [!tip] Describes how Eights deal with outer conflict in general.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/2025-04-08-Type_468_Harmony-1.svg" width="300" />
</div>

Eights belong to the Reactive group, which means they respond to conflict with intensity, immediacy, and directness. When outer conflict arises, Eights don't hold back - they meet it head-on and are often quick to challenge or confront whatever feels threatening to their autonomy, values, or integrity.

**“I will stand up for what’s right, and I won’t back down.”**

They may become outspoken, assertive, or even aggressive when faced with tension, pushing for resolution through action rather than discussion. Their reactions can be swift and intense, driven by a deep need to protect themselves or those they care about from feeling vulnerable or controlled.

Eights often struggle with:

- feeling threatened by external forces that challenge their power or independence
- believing that they must always be in control, especially when emotional vulnerability feels dangerous
- being quick to escalate situations, fearing that if they don’t assert themselves immediately, they might lose their sense of self or control

While this reactivity can protect them and others in dangerous situations, it can also push people away if it becomes overbearing or domineering. Eights want to protect and make an impact - but they must also learn to navigate their intensity without alienating those around them.

## Object Relations - Relationalist

> [!tip] How Eights see the world, and what they expect from relationships.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/2025-04-08-Type_258_Object-1.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- see the world as a place where power dynamics are constantly at play
    - _“I must remain strong to protect myself and those I care about”_
- **Eights never want to be vulnerable** or appear weak in their relationships
    - their approach is often direct and assertive, testing the strength of others and expecting loyalty in return

**Their self-value is based on their ability to maintain strength and control within relationships.**

Eights value relationships where they feel respected, trusted, and free to be their true selves without fear of being controlled or dominated. They need connections where mutual strength is recognized and where they can give as much as they take.

- expect people to show up for them and be loyal, even when challenges arise
- often use their own strength and power to protect others, believing that relationships should be based on mutual protection and honesty
- can be fiercely protective of loved ones, but only if they feel those people are capable of standing up for themselves as well

For Eights, relationships must allow space for independence and strength. They need to feel they can protect themselves and others, but also require those around them to offer the same level of support and trust.

# The Paths of the Eight

> [!attention] Each Type in the Enneagram is connected by two lines to other types
> 
> **This can have two possible meanings:**
> 1. One line connects to the type which the person has left behind or repressed in childhood, while the second line connects to the type which the person may grow into
> 2. When we are at our best, we integrate into a “higher” type, whereas we go to a “lower” type when we’re under stress

## Path of Integration: [[Type 2 - The Helper]]

> [!tip] Eights become _open-hearted_ and _caring_, like Twos.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/2025-04-08-Type_8_Integration-1.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- **Eights learn to soften their approach**, becoming more vulnerable and open-hearted, embracing the emotional needs of others and themselves
    - they recognize the importance of compassion and empathy, and they begin to show care and affection in a more genuine way
- **they become more attuned to the emotional needs** of those around them, offering warmth and support without trying to control or dominate
    - they grow in understanding the importance of mutual give-and-take in relationships, rather than just providing for others in a one-sided way
- **Eights learn to be more gentle**, shedding their tough exterior and allowing their natural warmth to come forward
- **their confidence shifts toward emotional openness**, embracing tenderness and love without fear of losing control

## Path of Disintegration: [[Type 5 - The Investigator]]

> [!tip] Eights become _secretive_ and _fearful_, like Fives.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/2025-04-08-Type_8_Disintegration-1.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- **Eights under stress can withdraw emotionally**, becoming more secretive and distant, avoiding close connections or vulnerability
    - they may retreat into their inner world and become absorbed in their own thoughts, losing touch with their external environment and relationships
- **may become more paranoid or fearful**, fearing that others will betray them or take advantage of their openness
- **Eights may feel the need to isolate themselves**, turning inward to gather more information or analyze situations from a place of detachment
    - may suppress their emotions in favor of intellectualizing or overthinking everything, rather than addressing the emotional discomfort they are experiencing
- **may also become more rigid** in their thinking, focusing on facts or analysis to avoid dealing with the emotional turmoil they are facing

## Levels of Development

The Development Levels (or Health Levels) are an ever-moving system. During bad times in our lives, we will naturally drift into more unhealthy levels, eventually returning back to healthier levels once we feel better. Unlike with Integration & Disintegration, where traits are borrowed from other types, these traits originate solely from the starting type.

### Healthy Eights

> [!success] Powerful leaders who drive change, support loved ones, and find fulfillment through their caring nature.
- lead causes and communities with strength and confidence
- improve society and themselves
- offer refuge and advice to loved ones
- find gratitude and joy by developing their caring side

### Average Eights

> [!caution] Begin to dominate their environment, including others: want to feel that others are behind them, supporting their efforts.
- competitive and may view friendships or business relations as a battlefield, always looking for the next challenge to win
- assertive and stubborn, but also self-confident and competent
- during down time, they critically evaluate their actions and work towards self-improvement
- avoid showing vulnerability, which can be a roadblock for connection and intimacy
- confidence and stamina lifts them to new heights, with failure serving as a kick for more effort

### Unhealthy Eights

> [!danger] Develop delusional ideas about their power, invincibility, and ability to prevail - Megalomania.
- can become tyrannical and intimidating, scaring others off at first glance
- become addicted to the pursuit of power, will destroy anything blocking their way with fury
- feelings and emotions of others become insignificant, as they become blindfolded to the softer side of the human psyche
- may use empty threats to regain power over others and turn existing relationships into tests - where one can only pass or fail
    - may force themselves into loneliness

# A Deeper Look

> [!quote] ~The Wisdom of the Enneagram
> “Most Eights have told us that they felt that they had to become "adults" at an early age, perhaps to help bring in money to raise the other children in the family because of an absent father or some other calamity. Eights tend to grow up quickly, and survival issues are foremost to them, as if they were asking, ‘How can I - and the few people I care about - survive in a cruel, uncaring world?’“

## Definition of Anger

Anger represents a fundamental response to perceived threats, injustice, or unmet needs. It is an intense feeling that arises when boundaries are crossed, expectations are not met, or when a person feels blocked from achieving what they desire.

The following aspects can be observed, when talking about Anger:

- **Signal of Distress**: Anger often indicates that something is wrong, whether it's an external situation or an internal conflict.
- **Desire for Change**: Anger typically arises from a desire to correct a wrong or to assert oneself in the face of opposition.

Anger typically also generates a surge of energy, which can be directed constructively or destructively. This energy can fuel determination and courage or, if uncontrolled, lead to harmful behaviors.

---

## Central Feeling of Anger & Connection to Lust

Eights experience a deep, underlying **anger** that arises from feeling **vulnerable**, weak, or controlled by others. This anger often stems from their need to protect themselves and assert control over their environment, which they view as unpredictable and sometimes oppressive. Their core fear is being **dominated**, controlled, or powerless, which leads them to become fiercely independent and resistant to anyone or anything that might threaten their autonomy.

To manage this **anger**, Eights channel it into **lust** - a passionate, all-consuming drive to pursue life with intensity, strength, and power. **Lust** is the expression of their desire to **take charge** and **assert dominance** over their world. For Eights, it’s not just about **wanting** things; it’s about **owning** them, having full control, and pursuing life with a sense of **invincibility**.

- The **anger** inside Eights arises from their perception that the world is **unfair**, that others may seek to control them, or that they may not be able to protect themselves from harm. This often leads them to confront life head-on, using their **forceful energy** to push back against any perceived threat or limitation.
- **Lust**, in this context, is about **intensity** - the overwhelming drive to live fully, to **experience power**, and to control their environment. It’s a way for Eights to express their **strength**, both emotionally and physically, as a means of **protecting themselves** and making sure they are never at the mercy of others.

> [!danger] The passion of the Eight is **Lust**: an intense craving for control, power, and experience, which stems from a fear of being weak or vulnerable.

> _"If I don’t take charge, no one else will."_

- **Healthy Lust** allows Eights to **assert themselves** confidently, take **initiative**, and approach life with **passion**, all while maintaining a healthy **awareness** of their own vulnerabilities and the needs of others.

- **Unhealthy Lust** leads Eights to become **overbearing**, **domineering**, and constantly in pursuit of **control**, even when it’s unnecessary or damaging. Their anger fuels this drive for power, which can make them appear intimidating or aggressive to others.

> [!danger] The more insecure Eights are, the more they **push others away** through forceful behavior and aggression, often masking their own vulnerability. Their anger becomes an **armor** they wear to protect themselves from being hurt.

- The **fear of being controlled** or weak leads Eights to pursue an **excessive desire for strength**, often disregarding the impact this has on others. They can become **obsessed with gaining control**, often taking on too many challenges or trying to dominate every situation.
- This **lust for power** can sometimes be a way for Eights to prove to themselves and others that they are **untouchable**, that no one can hurt them or take away their autonomy. However, it can also lead to isolation, as their intensity and force can alienate those around them.

---

## From Power to Compassion: Embracing Vulnerability

> [!quote] Brene Brown
> “Vulnerability is not winning or losing; it’s having the courage to show up and be seen when we have no control over the outcome.”

True strength for Type 8s is not about dominance or control, but about the courage to be vulnerable, to open up to others, and to allow themselves to be seen without the armor of power.

> [!missing] Excessive Control ⟶ Emotional Isolation

The desire to stay in control or avoid vulnerability can lead to emotional isolation. By resisting the openness that comes with vulnerability, Type 8s may unknowingly distance themselves from others and miss out on deeper, more authentic connections.

> [!missing] Excessive Vulnerability ⟶ Loss of Boundaries

While embracing vulnerability is crucial, it’s important for Type 8s to recognize the need for healthy boundaries. Overexposing themselves without protecting their emotional well-being can lead to burnout or manipulation by others.

> [!check] Cultivate compassionate power. True power lies in embracing both strength and softness-balancing the courage to protect others with the vulnerability to ask for help when needed and connect authentically.

# Subtypes

## The Wings

> [!tip] Enneagram wings enrich primary type insights.
> It is believed that wings represent related personality styles which we can transition into, in order to develop new facets of ourselves. Many people identify strongly with the description of one or both of their wings, in addition to their primary type.

What mainly differentiates the wings from each other, is how they approach the common goal of the stereotypical Eight:

### 8w7 - The Nonconformist

- Advocate for the rights of others
- Think practically and creatively
- Be afraid of losing their autonomy
- Dream big and be idealistic
- Struggle to be emotionally honest or vulnerable

### 8w9 - The Diplomat

- Dislike taking orders from other people
- Be more calm and laid-back than 8w7s
- Struggle to openly share emotions
- Fear being controlled by others
- Seek autonomy and independence

## The Instinctual Variants

> [!tip] Due to their Instinctual Variant, a type might look like another.
> It may occur that an Enneagram type appears different on the surface, because they exhibit opposing behavioural patterns than you’d might expect at first. This can be due to their Instinctual Variant - their interaction may seem like a different type, but they do not use the approach to emotions from that type.

### sp - The Bastion

> [!info] Focuses on our needs for material supplies and security, including food, warmth and family relations.

<mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6; color: #ffffff;">May seem like </mark>

> "I take care of mine."

- focused on practical matters
- works to ensure own and loved ones' well-being
- tends to be materialistic, seeking money for power and prized possessions
- can become workaholic and worry about protecting possessions and investments
- may also become bullies and thieves, acting selfishly without regard for others
    - undermine or attack others to protect interests and material security

### so - The Comrade

> [!info] Focuses on our needs for belonging and membership within the larger group and community.

<mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6; color: #ffffff;">May seem like </mark>

> "Loyalty is everything."

- **Countertype:**
- prioritize powerful bonds with others based on honor and trust
- test people they care about and seek predictable friends who accept them
- relax by hosting social events, wining and dining friends, and sharing adventures
- enjoy debates about politics, sports, or religion

### sx - The Heartfire

> [!info] Focuses on our intimate relationships & friendships, as well as the vitality of our life force.

<mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6; color: #ffffff;">May seem like </mark>

> "You’re mine."

- are quietly intense and charismatic
    - have a sly sense of humor and enjoy being "bad."
- passionate about their loved ones and often seek to have a significant impact on the lives of those around them
- can be deeply loving and devoted, but may also view intimacy as a struggle for control
- stimulated by a good argument and can be impatient with niceness.
    - may be competitive for the thrill of it.
- may demand loyalty and consistency, and try to mold others into shapes that fit their needs and plans

# Growth as an Eight

1. **Check in with what you’re thinking and feeling before making decisions**
	As a body-based type, Eights act instinctively on their emotions, which can result in impulsive outbursts or decisions that aren’t fully thought through. When you feel the impulse to react, take time to sort through your thoughts and feelings before taking action.

2. **Learn that vulnerability is strength, not weakness**
	Eights see their more raw and vulnerable emotions as weak, but growth happens when they learn to see the value in these aspects of their personality. Vulnerability allows Eights to connect more deeply with others, and grow into their highest self.

3. **Discover your limits**
	Energetic Eights frequently push themselves to their limits, unaware they are doing so. This may cause them to unknowingly push other people to their limits as well. Pay more attention to your mental and emotional states, and allow yourself time to rest and recover when needed.

4. **Make an effort to give love to and receive love from others more openly**
	Eights tend to view other people as either with them or against them. This can make it difficult to form solid, authentic relationships. Be open to giving and receiving love more freely. Not everything has to be earned.

5. **Allow other people to take the lead (sometimes)**
	With their commanding presence and direct approach, the Eight makes a great leader. But there are times when you need to step back and allow someone else to step up to the plate. It’s important for Eights to learn when it’s appropriate to let other people take charge.
