# The Stereotypical Four

Fours feel that they are unlike other human beings, and consequently, that no one can understand them or love them adequately. They often see themselves as uniquely talented, possessing special, one-of-a-kind gifts, but also as uniquely disadvantaged or flawed. More than any other type, Fours are acutely aware of and focused on their personal differences and deficiencies.

> [!cite] “I’m just not like everyone else.“

- distinctive inner and outer presentation
- strong sense of identity & passionate about self-expression
- quirky and endearing
- may feel a sense of emptiness

---

## Talking Style - Lamenting

Self-Talk: _**“I’m feeling…”**_

- Longing, lamenting & yearning
- poetic turns of phrase, self-expression

---

# Where do Four come from?

## Childhood Wounds

Fours feel that they are not like their parents. Many Fours report fantasizing that they were mistakenly switched at the hospital, or that they are orphans or some kind of changeling.

They often express this as feeling that they have not been "seen" by their parents, that they did not connect sufficiently with their parents or their parents with them. The result is that Fours believe that something must be profoundly wrong with them, launching them on a lifelong "search for self."

They feel abandoned and misunderstood by their parents and later by other significant people. As a result of this pattern, Fours respond powerfully to people who trigger their desire for mirroring, for being seen and appreciated for who they are.

Fours tend to play "hide and seek" with others - hiding from people, but hoping that their absence will be noticed. They attempt to remain mysterious and intriguing enough to attract someone who will notice them and redeem them with their love. But self-concealment and self-revelation alternate and can be expressed with such extremes of intensity and need that Fours inadvertently drive the longed-for rescuer away.

Until they can recognize this pattern and see the unrealistic expectations they put on their intimates, Fours run the risk of alienating others with their emotional demands.

# What makes up a Four?

> [!info] Everything is connected.
The Enneagram can roughly be divided into 4 different groups, consisting of 3 subgroups with 3 types within each of them. All types are connected in some way, even if they may seem completely different at first glance.

## Intelligence Group - Heart Triad

> [!tip] How Fours deal with their main emotion of **Shame** and core issue of **Envy**.

<div style="text-align: center;">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/triads/e234_triad.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- **Core Question:** _“With who am I in a relationship of some sorts, and what quality does this relationship have?”_
    - **Issue:** not be seen, loved enough or not being cared for emotionally
- are also called “romantic” types due to their deep sensitivity and desire for authenticity and emotional depth

**Fours _internalize_ Shame, in order to cope with it.**

- tend to feel fundamentally flawed or “different,” and assume others are more whole or complete
    - they turn shame inward and use emotional intensity or self-expression to make sense of it
- seek significance, uniqueness, and a sense of being deeply understood by others

All Heart types (Types Two, Three & Four) depend on their emotional intelligence, to understand their own reactions and connect with others.

---

## Hornevian Group - Withdrawn Stance & Withdrawn Style

> [!tip] Describes how Fours get what they want and how they resolve inner conflicts.

![[../../99 - Meta/Attachments/2025-04-08-Type_459_Horney-1.svg|300]]

- introspective, self-aware, and emotionally deep
- withdraw not to avoid life, but to find meaning, process their inner world, or protect their sensitivity
- tend to pull away in times of stress, especially if they feel misunderstood or unseen
    - can become melancholic, absorbed in fantasy, or preoccupied with what’s missing

**These people retreat inward to preserve identity and protect emotional truth.**

Fours want authenticity and depth, and try to get it by withdrawing into their inner emotional life:
1. If they fully explore their inner experience, they believe they’ll discover what’s uniquely meaningful and find their true self. This often involves navigating intense emotions, both light and dark.
2. If others don’t “get” them, they may isolate or amplify their uniqueness in hopes of being seen for who they _really_ are.
3. If they feel abandoned or emotionally neglected, they retreat further - but often long for someone to reach in and find them there.

Fours prefer to resolve conflict or tension by going inward rather than pushing outward. They often process difficult emotions in private, hoping to emerge with insight or inspiration. This withdrawal can also become a form of self-soothing or self-protection - a way to maintain their identity when the world feels too shallow, cold, or indifferent.

---

## Harmony Group - Reactivity

> [!tip] Describes how Fours deal with outer conflict in general.

![[../../99 - Meta/Attachments/2025-04-08-Type_468_Harmony-1.svg|300]]

Fours belong to the Reactive (or “Emotional Realness”) group - types who respond to conflict with emotional depth, authenticity, and a desire for attunement. When tensions arise, Fours don’t minimize or dismiss them - they express their feelings and expect others to meet them there.

**“Staying positive will help to resolve the conflict.”**

They tend to bring emotional intensity into conflict situations, especially when they feel misunderstood, unseen, or dismissed. Instead of glossing over discomfort, they lean into it - hoping to find truth, resolution, or connection through full emotional expression.

Fours often struggle with:
- feeling abandoned, emotionally dismissed, or out of sync with others
- an internal sense of being “too much” _and_ “not enough” at the same time
- cycling between emotional expression and withdrawal when they don’t feel met

While this reactivity can deepen emotional bonds when others respond with presence and empathy, it can also push people away if it turns into volatility or idealized longing. Fours want truth in connection - not surface-level peace - and they’ll push for it, even if it stirs things up.

## Object Relations - Idealist

> [!tip] How Fours see the world, and what they expect from relationships.

<div>
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SnakeFist007/puzzle-of-personality/refs/heads/main/source/assets/triads/e147_object.svg" width="300" />
</div>

- often feel there is something deeply missing - in themselves, others, or the world
    - _“something essential isn’t here - maybe it never was”_
- they **never** want to settle for the ordinary, because it feels like a betrayal of something meaningful
    - long for ideal connection, deep resonance, and emotional authenticity

**Their self-value is based on staying true to a deeper sense of meaning.**

Fours tend to idealize relationships and experiences, seeking something special or transcendent. This often creates a tension between longing and dissatisfaction - the more they chase the ideal, the more the present moment feels inadequate.

- their sense of belonging is tied to being _seen as they truly are_ - unique, deep, and emotionally rich
- their sense of self often forms around what is missing or yearned for, not what is already present

The Four offers the “only” thing they feel truly defines them: their inner world. They focus on emotional truth, depth, and beauty - not only to connect with others, but to maintain a sense of identity that feels real and unshakable.

# The Paths of the Four

> [!attention] Each Type in the Enneagram is connected by two lines to other types
> 
> **This can have two possible meanings:**
> 1. One line connects to the type which the person has left behind or repressed in childhood, while the second line connects to the type which the person may grow into
> 2. When we are at our best, we integrate into a “higher” type, whereas we go to a “lower” type when we’re under stress

## Path of Integration: [[Type 1 - The Reformer]]

> [!tip] Fours become _objective_ and _principled_, like Ones.

![[../../99 - Meta/Attachments/2025-04-08-Type_4_Integration-1.svg|300]]

- **become more disciplined** and focused on doing what’s right, rather than just what feels authentic to them
    - they develop a strong sense of responsibility, balance, and fairness
- Fours learn to **channel their intense feelings** into constructive action, using their creative abilities to promote change in healthy, meaningful ways
- they develop the ability to step back, reflect, and see situations from a more rational perspective    
    - they begin to assess their feelings objectively, gaining a clearer understanding of how to improve both themselves and their environment

- **focus on integrity** and learn to live up to their personal ideals without becoming consumed by emotion
    - grounded in their personal values, Fours take more responsibility for their actions and decisions        
- Fours transform their **passion and individuality** into a constructive force that helps them and others live more fully and authentically

## Path of Disintegration: [[Type 2 - The Helper]]

> [!tip] Fours become _over-involved_ and _clinging_, like Twos.

![[../../99 - Meta/Attachments/2025-04-08-Type_4_Disintegration-1.svg|300]]

- when under stress, Fours can become **excessively focused on the needs of others**, losing sight of their own emotional needs
    - they may become overly dependent on others’ validation to feel valued or worthy
- start to **seek affirmation** and affection in ways that can feel smothering or needy to others
    - they may try to win love by becoming the caretaker or helper, expecting emotional support in return for their efforts
- **over-identify with their role as the “emotional savior”** and feel frustrated when others don’t appreciate or reciprocate their emotional investments
    - may become resentful of others’ lack of appreciation or affection
- Fours may feel **ignored or neglected** when their emotional intensity is not acknowledged, which leads to efforts to control the attention of others through their emotional needs

## Levels of Development

The Development Levels (or Health Levels) are an ever-moving system. During bad times in our lives, we will naturally drift into more unhealthy levels, eventually returning back to healthier levels once we feel better. Unlike with Integration & Disintegration, where traits are borrowed from other types, these traits originate solely from the starting type.

### Healthy Fours

> [!success] Emotionally attuned creators who produce groundbreaking art and inspire others with their unique, visionary perspectives.
- create thought-provoking and groundbreaking works of art that shift perspectives toward the greater good
- ecognized as idea synthesizers who can help others rethink what art should be
- out-of-the-box thinking, self-actualized Individualists
- highly attuned to their complex well of emotions

### Average Fours

> [!caution] Interiorize everything, taking everything personally, can become self-absorbed and moody, unable to be spontaneous or to “get out of themselves”.
- let out their stress through a creative outlet and may bond with a community of like-minded people to gain inspiration and support along the way
- emotionally intense and introspective
- seekers of authenticity, sometimes at the expense of others’ patience and feelings
- may become hypersensitive to criticism yet firm to themselves
    - results in them actively seeking praise and flattery
- may be strongly offended if others try to copy or relate to their experiences

### Unhealthy Fours

> [!danger] Tormented by delusional self-contempt, self-reproaches, self-hatred, and morbid thoughts: everything is a source of torment.
- excessively moody, depressed and fragile
- can develop an extreme tendency to ruminate, which is intrusive to their natural creative energies
- may lose their grip on reality, resort to substance abuse
- search for the “missing piece”, leads down a never-ending spiral of dead ends and roundabouts
- prone to developing the belief that there’s something inherently “broken” about them

# A Deeper Look

> [!quote] ~The Wisdom of the Enneagram
> “Fours feel that they are not like their parents. They often express this as feeling that they have not been "seen" by their parents, that they did not connect sufficiently with their parents or their parents with them. The result is that Fours believe that something must be profoundly wrong with them, launching them on a lifelong ‘search for self.’”

## Definition of Shame

Shame is an unpleasant self-conscious emotion often associated with:

- feelings of pain, exposure & distrust ⟶ **sense of powerlessness & worthlessness**
- negative self-evaluation ⟶ **motivation to quit**

It arises when we notice a substantial flaw in ourselves and we want to hide because of it. You don’t want to show yourself, or want to be seen by anyone. Examples of shame include body-image issues, heritage, being poor or having an addiction.

---

## Central Feeling of Shame & Connection to Envy

Fours often feel a deep sense of **shame** due to their belief that they are fundamentally different from others, or that they are inherently flawed or deficient in some way. This feeling stems from an internalized belief that they are not enough as they are, which they can’t shake off, no matter how much they strive to be unique or exceptional.

- This deep-seated sense of inadequacy is compounded by **envy** - the painful awareness that others seem to have what they lack, whether it be love, acceptance, or success.
- Fours often feel **left out** or **misunderstood**, and this fuels their longing to find their true self, their "specialness" that will make them **worthy** of love and admiration.

Pride in the Four is an effort to reconcile their feelings of deficiency by asserting their **uniqueness** or **individuality**. However, their self-image often revolves around their **differences** - their belief that they are more sensitive, deeper, and more authentic than others.

> [!danger] The passion of the Four is **Envy**: a painful longing for what others possess and a belief that they are somehow incomplete or flawed.

> _"I am different from everyone else, but no one truly understands me."_

- **Healthy Envy** can lead to **authentic self-expression** and a deep connection with others who appreciate their unique perspective.
    - Fours learn that their uniqueness can be a gift when they embrace it, and that they do not need to compete with others to feel valuable.

- **Unhealthy Envy** can lead to **melancholy**, feelings of being disconnected, and an overemphasis on **how others have what they lack**.
    - This can manifest as self-pity, frustration, or withdrawing into their emotions as they feel they can never measure up to those around them.

> [!danger] The more insecure Fours feel, the more they try to highlight their **differences** and distance themselves from others to preserve their sense of uniqueness, often by adopting a **romanticized** or **exaggerated** identity.

- The **narrative of deficiency** leads them to feel **inadequate**, and they may struggle with **envying** others who appear to have a sense of belonging, love, or a more stable identity.
- In their search for identity, Fours may experience moments of **intense emotional volatility**, marked by a desire to be seen as **special**, **unique**, and different, but ultimately feeling unfulfilled.

---

## Navigate the Search for Identity

> [!quote] Anonymous
> “You are not your feelings. You are the one who experiences them.”

Recognizing that the search for identity is a journey, not a destination. Understanding that who they are is not solely defined by what they feel or what they lack.

> [!missing] Excessive Idealization ⟶ Disconnection from Reality

The tendency to romanticize certain qualities or experiences can lead to a distorted sense of self. This idealization creates expectations that are often unattainable, leading to dissatisfaction or frustration when they can’t live up to their ideals.

> [!missing] Excessive Disconnection ⟶ Feeling of Emptiness

Withdrawing into their unique sense of self can result in isolation or an exaggerated sense of being misunderstood. This sense of being “different” may contribute to feelings of emptiness or a lack of belonging.

> [!check] Embrace the fluidity of identity. Acknowledge that personal growth is a constant process, and true identity can evolve with experiences rather than being defined by past feelings or images of oneself.

# Subtypes

## The Wings

> [!tip] Enneagram wings enrich primary type insights.
> It is believed that wings represent related personality styles which we can transition into, in order to develop new facets of ourselves. Many people identify strongly with the description of one or both of their wings, in addition to their primary type.

What mainly differentiates the wings from each other, is how they approach the common goal of the stereotypical Two:

### 4w3 - The Enthusiast

**Hold an “active” approach, tend to be more extraverted.**

- Be energetic, distinctive, and driven
- Desire significance and legacy
- Seek uniqueness and individuality
- Fear having no impact on the world
- Engage with those around them

### 4w5 - The Free Spirit

**Hold an “passive” approach, tend to be more introverted.**

- Be creative, intellectual, and objective
- Seem reserved and withdrawn from others  
- Want to understand the world
- Seek personal impact and identity
- Fear having little knowledge or significance

## The Instinctual Variants

> [!tip] Due to their Instinctual Variant, a type might look like another.
> It may occur that an Enneagram type appears different on the surface, because they exhibit opposing behavioural patterns than you’d might expect at first. This can be due to their Instinctual Variant - their interaction may seem like a different type, but they do not use the approach to emotions from that type.

### sp - The Endurer

> [!info] Focuses on our needs for material supplies and security, including food, warmth and family relations.

> "My feelings are too much for the world to bear."

- **Countertype:**
- temper down their emotions, because they want to stay connected to people
    - least outwardly emotional 4, most anxious
- enduring & tenacious, go against their compulsions
- hope others see their sacrifice and appreciate them for it
    - but will never feel like they have accomplished enough
- want to connect with people
- driven to help others, to distract from their own pain

⟶ This Four may resemble a One, Three or Seven, because they suppress their emotions and focus on endurance and achievement.

### so - The Outsider

> [!info] Focuses on our needs for belonging and membership within the larger group and community.

> "Nobody gives me an extra chance."

- socially active and engaged
- see themselves as unique and different from others
- process the envy of "not fitting in" as a problem with themselves
    - wear their pain and "look like they're suffering"
    - sometimes are their own worst enemy
- hope to gain other people's support and attention by showing their pain
- can be overly sensitive, deeply connected to their own emotional world
- want to be understood and forgiven for their "issues", loved unconditionally

⟶ This is the “classic” Four!

### sx - The Firebrand

> [!info] Focuses on our intimate relationships & friendships, as well as the vitality of our life force.

> "Why does everything have to be an argument?"

- angriest person on the Enneagram
- competitive, compare themselves with the most popular person in the room, wanting to become that person
- projects their pain onto others, blame them as the reason for the pain
    - muted sense of envy, ignores their feeling of pain
- easily offended, people have to walk on eggshells around them or avoid them entirely
- most likely to confront people, can find themselves in a negative spiral after blowing up
    - aggressively shameless

⟶ This Four may resemble an Eight, due to dealing with their pain by expression of anger and confrontation.

### Blind Spot

> [!warning] Blind Spots  
> The instinct we neglect the most – our “blind” instinct – can create tension. The expression of this differs from where the blind spot lays.

# Growth as a Four

1. **Practice positive affirmations**
	Fours are prone to negative self-talk. By focusing your mind on positive thoughts, you can develop a kinder, more positive self-image and mindset.
	
2. **View yourself from another person’s perspective**
	Keep in mind that your feelings, though valid, are subjective. Emotions are not all you are, but a part of who you are. Try to view yourself more objectively, specifically looking at your positive traits and accomplishments.
	
3. **Consider the ways in which you are similar to those around you**
	Fours focus their attention on how they’re different from others. Instead, look for ways to connect with others. What do you have in common? Doing this will help you form deeper relationships.
	
4. **Start small**
	Fours may feel like they lack the confidence and discipline to get things done. If what you want to accomplish feels overwhelming, break it up into small, achievable steps.
	
5. **Learn to be open to constructive feedback - positive and negative**
	It’s essential for Fours to get out of their heads and learn to accept feedback from the outer world. Understand that negative feedback isn’t an attack on who you are as a person. And positive feedback isn’t something to dismiss or devalue. There are important lessons to be learned from constructive feedback.
